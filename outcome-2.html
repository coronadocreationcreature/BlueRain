Learning outcome 02 · Ethics & society

Ethics, society, and responsible AI.

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

Learning space. Examine who benefits from AI systems, who may be harmed, and how design choices shape their effects.

Questions to guide my learning

  • How can bias enter a dataset or an AI system’s design?
  • What happens when an automated decision is difficult to explain or challenge?
  • How might AI affect privacy, work, access, and power?

My reflection

There are certain ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications. These issues include algorithmic bias, which can unintentionally target minorities or discriminate based on past data unfairly. Another of these issues is dual use, which is when an AI system is built for good and has practical use but is misused and can be manipulated for malpractice as not intended by the designer of the system. An example of algorithmic bias was the predicting policing pilot being used for criminal justice.

In the city of Fairmont they use an AI system called SafeZone that is made to be unbiased and data driven to reduce crime by directing police towards flagged areas. However, due to past historically over-policed areas being mainly minority neighborhoods, this led to overpolicing in these areas. This reinforced a negative feedback loop that kept increasing arrests and patrolling in minority neighborhoods. When AI systems are given data that could be skewed from previous human bias, they can unintentionally worsen the proclaimed neutral and balanced system. This is called algorithmic bias, which is when an algorithm is based on biased data which leads to more biased decisions even if it is not explicitly programmed or designed that way. Algorithmic bias hurts minorities and previously targeted groups who fall victim to the reinforced patterns of previous discrimination.

Another ethical issue associated with AI systems is dual use which is when AI systems that are designed for good can be misused for ill intent. An example of this was the protein folding breakthrough using biotech. An open source model called ProteoGen that predicted how protein folds interact with human cells. This was a great use of an AI system and was a breakthrough for developing treatment for a genetic disorder that has been harming countless children. However, although this was the intended use and served great purpose, a dual use was being developed by a watchdog group to harness modifications with bioweapon applications.

Dual use when it comes to AI systems’ ethics is when a system is deliberately exploited in a way the designer of the system didn’t anticipate. This hurts both the clients who could lose access to the system if it is shut down for security reasons but also those who could be harmed by the misuse of the AI. This affects the community for which it was inherently designed to help as they can lose access to the system and its benefits. The dual use falls ethically more on the people misusing the AI tools and systems as they are exploiting it not as intended to hurt a group. The AI systems are designed with good intentions but it is up to the client and those who can access the systems ethically to not take advantage of them or misuse them for malice.
